# Abstract
Influenza surveillance is essential because seasonal influenza imposes a substantial public health burden. Influenza outbreak patterns vary across regions, underscoring the need to account for regional heterogeneity. Wastewater-Based Epidemiology (WBE) has emerged as a cost-effective tool for monitoring influenza outbreaks. However, assessing the associations between influenza outpatient cases and wastewater viral concentration is challenging because the relationship may be nonlinear and time-varying, while spatio-temporal heterogeneity must be considered. To address these challenges, we applied a nonparametric additive model, which accommodates linear and nonlinear effects while offering interpretable results. Within the model for South Korea, region-specific temporal effects accounted for most of the variation in influenza outpatient cases, while wastewater viral concentration provided additional explanatory information. Specifically, the effects of wastewater viral concentrations were relatively strong during the winter influenza epidemic season and the subsequent resurgence in cases. These findings highlight the potential of WBE for cost-efficient and real-time influenza surveillance.
